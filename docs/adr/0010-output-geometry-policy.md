# Output geometry policy — world-cubic chunks and an anisotropy-aware pyramid

**Status:** Accepted (2026-08-24); amended by [Follow-up (issue #85)](#follow-up-issue-85), which refines the isotropy yardstick, the axis floor and the depth stop where implementation found the rule below underdetermined.

Zarrmony plans an output store's [geometry](../../CONTEXT.md) itself instead of delegating chunk shape to `bioio-ome-zarr`'s memory-target heuristic. Chunks are the largest power-of-two shape whose raw size fits a **512 KiB** target and which is closest to cubic **in micrometres**, computed per level from that level's physical voxel spacing. The pyramid halves every spatial axis whose spacing is within `isotropy_tolerance` (default `1.5`) of the finest axis, so levels move toward isotropy and the scarce axis is spent last. Pyramid depth is the **greater** of the existing `pyramid_min_size=256` Y/X rule and the depth at which a level becomes a coarse level — `Z·Y·X·itemsize ≤ 64 MiB` and `max(Y, X) ≤ 2048` — with an axis floor of 32 voxels and no halving of an axis already below it. Mean-pool remains the downsampler; `downsample_method` exposes `"max"` for sparse-label acquisitions. The policy applies uniformly to per-scene, bf2raw and plate output, and is carried by a single frozen `Geometry` object rather than by loose keyword arguments; `chunk_shape` and `pyramid_min_size` survive on `convert()` as retained sugar that folds into it. Sharding is deliberately not implemented. This ADR is decisions-only; implementation is unsliced.

The three bounds borrowed here — 64 MiB per (t,c), 2048 long axis, 32-voxel floor — are Lucida's `SourceCoarseConfig` defaults, adopted knowingly (see Consequences) and exposed as `Geometry` fields so a store can be planned for a different consumer.

## Why these numbers

Every constant traces to a measurement rather than to taste. The reference dataset is a SmartSPIM whole-brain acquisition, `(1, 3, 3627, 8835, 7452)` uint16 at Z 2.0 / Y 1.8 / X 1.8 µm — 1.30 TiB raw, ~283 GB converted.

- **512 KiB raw chunk target.** Two measured constraints meet there. Lucida's transport study (`docs/research/source-read-concurrency.md`) puts interactive source reads at **p50 325 KiB** with a flat **~110 ms TTFB** per object, and flags ~5 MB objects as bandwidth-bound rather than round-trip-bound; sampling the converted store's own level-0 chunks gives blosc-zstd ratios of **2.3–4.2× on tissue**, so 512 KiB raw lands at ~150–220 KiB compressed, near that band. On the other side, `MAIN_VIEW_UPLOAD_BUDGET_BYTES` is 8 MB of _decoded_ bytes per frame, so 512 KiB decoded admits 16 chunks per frame.
- **What the old default did.** With `chunk_shape=None`, `multiscale_chunk_size_from_memory_target` fills the rightmost axis first under a 16 MiB budget, producing `[1,1,1,1125,7452]` at level 0 — a full-width, single-Z-plane slab of 15.99 MiB. One chunk exceeds a whole frame's upload budget (0.48 chunks/frame), and because the chunk spans the entire X extent, frustum culling has exactly one column to choose from and can never trim it.
- **The size of the defect.** Fetching a 512³-voxel region at the centre of the volume costs 1,024 chunks and 17.2 GB to deliver 268 MB of wanted voxels — **1.6 % efficiency**. At 64³ it costs 512–729 chunks and 268–382 MB, ~70–100 %.
- **The clearest statement of it.** Lucida's admission window is `max(64, 4 × concurrency) = 64` requests, drained centre-out. Under the old geometry those 64 requests buy 64 whole-brain Z planes — 128 µm of Z across the full 15.9 × 13.4 mm, almost all off-screen. At 64³ the same 64 requests buy a 256³ cube: **461 × 461 × 512 µm centred on the camera**.

## Considered Options

- **Keep delegating chunk shape to `bioio-ome-zarr`'s memory-target heuristic.** Rejected. The heuristic is not wrong so much as dimensionally blind: it grows the rightmost axis first under a byte budget, which is the right answer for a 2D plane and the wrong answer for anything with a Z extent. On a wide volume it always converges on full-width single-plane slabs, and the failure is silent — the store validates, opens in napari, and only misbehaves under a 3D camera. Nothing about the heuristic can be tuned to avoid this from the caller's side; the only lever is `chunk_shape`, which forces the caller to compute per-level shapes by hand. Owning the planner is the smaller maintenance burden.
- **Fixed voxel-cubic chunks (`64³` everywhere), clamped to the level shape.** Rejected, though it captures most of the win — against 1.6 % efficiency it is a 40–60× improvement, and on near-isotropic data it is exactly what the chosen rule returns. It fails on anisotropic acquisitions: a 10:1 confocal stack (Z 5 µm, XY 0.5 µm) gets a chunk spanning 320 × 32 × 32 µm, so culling granularity is ten times coarser in Z than laterally and asking for one 5 µm plane drags in 320 µm of stack. That is the same defect this ADR exists to remove, one order of magnitude down. Choosing it would also be internally inconsistent: the pyramid rule below treats µm/voxel as the thing that decides which axis is scarce, and there is no principled reason for fetch granularity to ignore the same fact. The world-cubic rule degenerates to `64³` whenever the data is near-isotropic, so nothing is lost on the common case.
- **Halve Z, Y and X uniformly at every level (`SPATIAL_DIMS = {"Z", "Y", "X"}`).** Rejected. It is a one-line change and it is identical to the chosen rule on the reference dataset, which makes it tempting. But it preserves anisotropy as an invariant ratio: a 10:1 stack is still 10:1 at every level, so its coarse level is a stack of slabs rather than an overview volume — again the reported symptom, relocated to the coarse tier. Worse, it halves the scarce axis at the same rate as the abundant one. In light-sheet and confocal, XY is oversampled and Z is undersampled; uniform halving destroys the information there is least of, fastest. Anisotropy-aware downsampling is the established convention for such stacks (Neuroglancer precomputed, BigDataViewer/N5 per-level factors), not a local invention. Its cost is real but bounded: on strongly anisotropic data the pyramid needs one or two extra levels, taking overhead from ~1.14× to ~1.33× of level 0, and on near-isotropic data it costs nothing.
- **Lower `pyramid_min_size` (256 → 64) rather than adding an overview-target stopping rule.** Rejected. It is one constant and it would produce a small enough level on the reference dataset — but only by accident, and it overshoots to a 1.8 MiB level where a 13.8 MiB one was needed. The deeper problem is that a Y/X voxel-count floor has no relationship to the property being sought. "Does a level exist that a viewer can hold whole?" is a question about decoded bytes and lateral extent, and any proxy for it will be right on the shapes it was tuned against and wrong elsewhere. Tying depth to the actual bound also makes the guarantee checkable at conversion time instead of discoverable in a viewport.
- **Apply `min_size` to `min(Z, Y, X)` once Z becomes a downsampled axis.** Rejected — it is the obvious generalisation and it is a regression. A 3-plane × 2048² stack has `min = 3`, below any sensible floor, so the pyramid would collapse to a single level. Such data is safe today only because Z is excluded from `SPATIAL_DIMS`. Hence the axis floor is per-axis and an axis already below it simply never halves, and hence depth is a `max()` of two rules rather than a replacement of one by the other — which also makes the change monotone: no existing conversion loses a level.
- **Max-pool the whole pyramid.** Rejected. It is what Lucida's server-side generated coarse tier already does (`DOWNSAMPLE_ALGORITHM_VERSION = "max-pool-v1"`), so adopting it would preserve the appearance users see today, and for sparse labels the difference is stark: a 15 µm soma occupies 1.6 % of one level-5 voxel, so mean-pooling renders it at 114 against a background of 100 while max-pooling renders it at 1000 against ~141. But max-pool biases every level above 0 high and makes the pyramid useless for measurement, and at high factors it lifts background toward the noise maximum — the max of 32,768 draws sits roughly 4σ out. Mean-pool is also what the OME-Zarr ecosystem assumes. The knob covers the sparse case without making it everyone's default.
- **Mean-pool the detail levels and max-pool only the coarse level.** Rejected as a default, and it is the strongest rejected option: it maps exactly onto Lucida's own two-tier model, where detail levels are for inspection and the coarse level is for navigation. But it makes the pyramid internally inconsistent, and a viewer with no coarse/detail concept — napari, vizarr — shows a visible brightness discontinuity at the moment it crosses into the last level. Shipping a default whose artefact appears only in third-party viewers is the wrong trade; a user who wants this can convert with `downsample_method="max"` throughout.
- **Gate the new policy on `Z > 1`, exempting 2D and plate output.** Rejected. It is the zero-regression choice for the HCS path and would have been defensible, but it leaves a real defect in place: a 2160² Phenix field currently gets a single `(1,1,1,2160,2160)` chunk of 8.9 MiB, which also exceeds a frame's upload budget. The objection to applying the policy — that splitting a 1080² field into nine chunks adds eight round trips — does not survive the concurrency measurement: at concurrency 16 those nine issue in parallel for one TTFB, they sit closer to the 325 KiB interactive profile than one 900 KiB object does, padded edge chunks compress to nearly nothing, and the field fills progressively rather than all-at-once. A dimensionality-dependent special case in the one place a single rule is most valuable is not worth buying a problem the measurements say does not exist.
- **Add five keyword arguments in the existing style rather than a policy object.** Rejected. Each geometry parameter currently touches ~14 sites — `convert()`'s signature, the audit dict, three dispatch calls, four internal writer helpers with eight pass-throughs, plus `cli.py`, `scene.py` and `plate.py`. Five new parameters is ~70 mechanical edits and a sixteen-parameter public signature, and the next geometry knob costs the same again. The policy object makes future additions one field. `chunk_shape` and `pyramid_min_size` are retained rather than removed, so no caller breaks.
- **Expose no new knobs; hardcode the constants.** Rejected. It conflicts with the smart-default-plus-override principle already applied across the codebase, and it would bake two numbers borrowed from another repository's configuration into zarrmony with no way to unborrow them — the one case where a knob is not optional.
- **Choose an independent overview target (e.g. 32 MiB) instead of adopting Lucida's 64 MiB.** Rejected. Margin sounds prudent but buys nothing: the bound is a `>` comparison, so a level at exactly 64 MiB passes, and on the reference dataset both targets select the same level. Deliberately diverging would risk planning a store that satisfies a self-imposed number while missing the real one. The coupling is instead made explicit and configurable.
- **Ship `shard_shape` as an opt-in `Geometry` field, defaulting to `None`.** Rejected for now, and this is the closest call in the ADR. The object count is the one number this policy makes materially worse — 87k to ~3.2 M for the reference store — and sharding answers it directly: a 256³ shard holds 64 chunks of 64³ and would bring that store to ~47k objects, a 68× reduction, with chunks still individually range-readable. The write side already exists upstream (`OMEZarrWriter` normalises shards per level and validates that each shard dimension divides its chunk dimension). It is rejected because `lucida-store`'s `parse_codec_chain` accepts only `[bytes]` or `[bytes, compressor]` and rejects any chain whose first codec is not `bytes` — which is precisely what a `sharding_indexed` array presents. An opt-in flag would therefore ship a trap: enabling it produces a store that fails to open with `first storage codec must be 'bytes', got 'sharding_indexed'`, an error that reads as corruption rather than as an unsupported feature. A flag shipped is a flag supported, tested and documented, and there is no consumer for it today. Revisit when Lucida gains `sharding_indexed` read support; the decision is then one field plus a default flip.
- **Build an OME-Zarr → OME-Zarr `zarrmony rechunk` command as part of this work.** Deferred to a separate issue. The migration is unusually cheap and provably lossless for stores written under the old default — because today's chunks are full-width single-plane slabs, rebuilding 64³ chunks means reading Z in bands of 64 where every input chunk is consumed exactly once, a sequential streaming pass at ~8.4 GB per channel-band, and the new pyramid mean-pools the same untouched level 0 so it yields the values a re-conversion would. That makes the tool worth building, but it is a distinct feature with its own surface — metadata-copy fidelity, `--force` semantics, resumability across a multi-hour job over millions of objects — and bundling it would delay the geometry change. The reference dataset is re-converted from source instead.

## Consequences

- **Level-0 chunks on the reference dataset go from `[1,1,1,1125,7452]` (15.99 MiB) to `[1,1,64,64,64]` (512 KiB).** Frustum culling gains real freedom on every axis; the per-frame upload budget admits 16 chunks where it previously admitted less than one.
- **The pyramid gains a level and shrinks.** Levels become `(3627,8835,7452) → (1813,4417,3726) → (906,2208,1863) → (453,1104,931) → (226,552,465) → (113,276,232)`. Because Z now halves too, each level is ⅛ of the previous rather than ¼, so levels 1–5 add ~14 % over level 0 where levels 1–4 previously added ~33 %. Smaller chunks compress somewhat worse than full-width slabs, so expect a wash to modestly smaller overall rather than a guaranteed saving.
- **Level 5 is a coarse level, and that switches off a server-side code path.** At 113 × 276 × 232 it is 13.8 MiB per (t,c) with a 276 long axis and 40 chunks, satisfying every `SourceCoarseConfig` bound; `select_source_coarse_level` returns it, `coarse_level_index` is populated, and `plan_generated_coarse_for_image` early-returns `None`, so Lucida stops generating its own max-pooled coarse tier at concurrency 1 over 1.3 TiB of source. Its appearance changes accordingly: the coarse tier becomes mean-pooled, which is dimmer for sparse structures.
- **Object count rises ~37×** on the reference dataset (87,048 → ~3.2 M; 2.78 M at level 0). Plate output rises ~4–5× — a 384-well plate at 4 fields × 3 channels goes from roughly 23k to roughly 106k objects. Irrelevant on local disk; on object storage this is listing and per-object metadata cost, and it is the pressure that will eventually reopen the sharding decision.
- **`NGFF` scale metadata needs no separate handling.** `OMEZarrWriter` derives `dataset_scales` as each level's shape divided by level 0's, per axis, so per-axis-varying downsample factors flow into `coordinateTransformations` automatically.
- **`build_pyramid` becomes shape-driven.** Deriving coarsen factors from consecutive entries of `level_shapes` rather than hardcoding `{i: 2}` makes uniform and per-axis-varying downsampling one code path.
- **Existing stores are unaffected and are not migrated.** New conversions get the new geometry; the reference dataset is re-converted from source. A `zarrmony rechunk` command is a follow-up issue.
- **The audit block records resolved geometry, not inputs.** Per-level shapes, per-level chunk shapes, the coarse level index and the downsample method replace the current `chunk_shape: None` / `pyramid_min_size: 256` pair, which was accurate and uninformative — the defect this ADR fixes was found by inspecting a viewport, not the store's own metadata.
- **A sharded store cannot be read by Lucida.** Recorded here so the constraint is not rediscovered: it is a codec-chain limitation in `lucida-store`, not a zarrmony limitation, and `bioio-ome-zarr` supports the write side today.
- **SemVer.** A minor bump. On-disk geometry changes for every new conversion, including plate output, but no API breaks — `chunk_shape` and `pyramid_min_size` keep working. The CHANGELOG should carry a migration note for users comparing new stores against old ones, and should name the object-count increase explicitly for anyone hosting on object storage.

## Follow-up (issue #85)

Implementing the pyramid rule surfaced three shapes the decision above does not settle. All three refine "halve every spatial axis whose spacing is within `isotropy_tolerance` of the finest axis, subject to an axis floor of 32 voxels"; none of them changes the reference dataset's levels, and each exists to keep the rule from misfiring on a shape the summary paragraph did not have in view. They are recorded here rather than edited into that paragraph so the decision as accepted stays legible next to what implementing it cost.

- **The isotropy yardstick is the finest _still-halvable_ axis, not the finest axis outright.** An axis pinned at its floor holds its level-0 spacing forever while every other axis doubles theirs each level, so leaving it in the comparison set lets an axis that can no longer be spent go on deciding what the others may spend. Take a `(1, 1, 1, 256, 256)` field at Z 1.0 / YX 0.5 µm: Z is frozen at one plane, and by level 2 the laterals have reached 2.0 µm — a ratio of 2.0 against the frozen Z, outside the 1.5 tolerance — so `(32, 32)` is never written. That is every 2D acquisition losing the tail of its pyramid, which is the one shape this ADR's monotonicity promise most needs to protect. Restricting the yardstick to axes the floor still permits to halve is the fix; it is a no-op wherever nothing is frozen, which includes the reference dataset. Found by an existing test failing, not by inspection — the interaction is invisible in the rule as stated because "the finest axis" reads as a property of the data rather than of the level.
- **Depth also stops once Y and X are both at their floor.** The rule as accepted halves any eligible axis and stops on the Y/X `pyramid_min_size` test, which asks whether the laterals got _smaller_ — it cannot fire when they are not moving at all. A 512-plane column over a 128² field at 0.5 µm isotropic therefore reaches `(128, 32, 32)`, freezes both laterals, and then keeps halving Z alone: four more levels that are new resolutions in name only, each one _increasing_ anisotropy, which is backwards for a rule whose whole purpose is to reduce it. So the pyramid also stops when neither lateral can ever halve again. The phrasing matters — it asks what Y and X can _ever_ do, not what they did this level — because a stack with oversampled Z legitimately spends Z alone for a level or two while the laterals wait for its spacing to catch up: `(256, 1024, 1024)` at Z 0.2 / YX 1.0 µm shrinks Z alone twice, and only then do all three axes halve together. This is a ceiling on depth, so it composes with rather than replaces the `max()` of the two depth rules. At the default `pyramid_min_size=256` it is unreachable (256 > 32, so the depth rule always bites first); it becomes load-bearing when a caller lowers the depth floor toward the axis floor, and again when the coarse-level stopping rule starts extending depth past the Y/X rule.
- **On Y and X the axis floor is capped by `pyramid_min_size`.** The two floors overlap on the lateral axes, and under any default policy the depth rule stops the pyramid long before Y or X could reach 32. The cap therefore only bites when a caller has deliberately asked for a floor _below_ 32 — and a caller passing `pyramid_min_size=8` is entitled to the levels they asked for rather than to a borrowed 32-voxel default silently overriding them. Reading the axis floor as an absolute would have cost levels on existing conversions, which is exactly the monotonicity the "Apply `min_size` to `min(Z, Y, X)`" rejection above commits to. Z keeps the uncapped 32, since nothing else bounds it.

Two things this slice did **not** change, both deliberate: pyramid depth is still the `pyramid_min_size` Y/X rule alone — the coarse-level stopping rule, and with it `coarse_max_bytes` and `coarse_max_long_axis`, is a later slice and those `Geometry` fields stay inert — and `downsample_method` is still unread, so mean-pool is the only downsampler in the code.
